This is a translation for your convenience. The French version prevails.

Déjà Nous is a game for couples. What happens between you two is none of our business: your answers, your first names and your “Book of us” stay on your phone, and we never receive them. We only process what we need to open the full game you bought, and to reply when you write to us. This page says what, why, and for how long.

Who is responsible

The data controller is Philippe Delvigne EI, trading as “Déjà Nous Games”, 9 chemin Commanderie Saint-Jean-de-Malte, 13080 Aix-en-Provence, France.

For any question about your data: contact@dejanous.love.

In short

  • No account, no ads, no advertising trackers, no sale of data.
  • Your answers and “The Book of us” stay on your phone. They are not sent to any of our servers.
  • The full game is bought in the app, through Google Play. We receive neither your name, nor your e-mail address, nor your payment details.
  • To open the full game, our server has Google confirm your purchase, then keeps a fingerprint of the purchase token for 24 hours. Nothing else.
  • The texts of the full game carry an invisible mark derived from your purchase.
  • The website sets no cookies.

What stays on your phone

Settings, games in progress, the connection history, vouchers won and “The Book of us” (answers kept, memories, words found together, rounds of “Two truths, one lie”) are stored in the app’s private space, on your phone only. We have no access to them.

This data is kept in two copies on the device to survive updates. Android’s automatic backup may include it in your phone’s Google backup, depending on your settings. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data removes it from the device.

The app counts a few numbers for you (games started and finished, evenings, time spent in the app) and shows them in its settings.

Anonymous app statistics. At most once a day, the app sends us these counters, added since the last sending, with its version number and edition. Never a first name, an answer, a card, a date of play or any identifier of the phone: nothing tells who is playing. It is on when the app is installed, said on the very first screen, and turns off in one tap in Settings ("Share anonymous statistics").

Purpose
Knowing which games and evenings people enjoy, to improve the game. Nothing else: no advertising, no profile.
Legal basis
Our legitimate interest in measuring how the app is used, without identifying anyone.
Data
The counters above, as totals. Our server writes neither the IP address nor anything that points to the phone.
Where
At Cloudflare, our host, then in our private archive at GitHub.
Retention
25 months, then only daily totals remain.

The app has no account, no ads and no advertising trackers.

Playing on two phones

Two-phone games use the direct connection between nearby phones (Bluetooth and Android’s “Nearby” service). The choices needed for the game go directly from one phone to the other, without going through any of our servers.

For this, the app asks for the Bluetooth, nearby devices and location permissions: Android requires location to find a nearby phone. The app only checks that location is turned on; it does not read your position and does not record it. The notification permission is used to tell you when your partner is waiting, and for the evening reminder if you choose one.

The Thread, invitations and gifted evenings

With “The Thread”, you answer a question on your phone, then the app prepares a message that you send with your own messaging app (WhatsApp, SMS, e-mail or copy and paste). The link holds the card number, your first name (and your gender, for French grammar), your answer and your guess, the other person’s when they reply, and a technical marker of the app that made it.

The answers are scrambled so they cannot be read at a glance. This is not encryption: anyone who receives the link can recover them. A Snap! “timed challenge” travels the same way (card numbers, first names and genders, true-or-false answers, time of each answer), never with the text of a card.

An invitation sent from the app, to play on your partner’s phone, holds the language, your first names (and genders, if you gave them), your couple’s stage and a secret that links your two phones for two-phone play.

A gifted evening holds your note, your first name, the evening chosen and the opening day if you chose one.

In these three kinds of link, the data sits after the “#” sign. Your browser never sends it to the website’s server: the page only reads it on the phone that opens it.

An invitation link from the website’s demo holds, in its address, a card number and, if you choose, your hidden answers (up to three choices) and your first name. Like any page address, it goes through the website’s host; the website keeps no record of it.

No Déjà Nous server keeps these messages. They go through the messaging app you choose, under its own terms.

To link the steps of one visit, the tab draws a random number, erased when it closes: it never links two visits and says nothing about you. Nothing is sent when your browser asks not to be tracked (Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track).

Buying and opening the full game

The purchase. The full game is bought in the app, through Google Play. Google processes the payment under its own privacy policy. It never passes your payment details on to us. As with any publisher, Google Play gives us sales reports (order number, date, product, amount, country); we keep them as accounting records.

Opening it. The texts of the full game are encrypted in the app. To open them, the app sends our server api.dejanous.love:

  • the purchase token Google Play gave it;
  • the name of the card vault of this version of the app;
  • the fingerprint of the certificate that signs the app, to check that it is the official app.

It sends neither your name, nor your e-mail, nor your answers. The server asks Google whether this token matches a valid purchase of the full game. If it does, it sends back the key that opens the cards. This key is stored nowhere on the server: it is computed again for each request. Your phone keeps it, and the full game then opens offline.

Purpose
Checking the purchase and delivering the key of the full game.
Legal basis
Performance of the contract: supplying the game you bought.
Data
The purchase token, read and passed to Google for checking, never stored. A fingerprint of that token (part of its SHA-256), stored so that Google is not asked at every request. The fingerprint of the app’s certificate and the name of the vault, read and not kept.
Retention
The token fingerprint: 24 hours, then deleted automatically. Google Play sales reports: 10 years, the retention period for accounting records.

The mark in the texts of the full game

Every copy of the full game is personal. When its texts open on your phone, the app adds a mark derived from your purchase, in two ways:

  • an invisible signature, made of zero-width characters, placed just before the final punctuation of each text;
  • on a few cards, a detail that changes from one purchase to another, without changing the meaning of the card.

The mark is the beginning of a fingerprint of your Google Play purchase token. It is computed on your phone: nothing is sent for it. It changes neither the meaning of the cards nor the way you play.

If texts of the game are shared (a file, a screenshot, a copy of the app, a hand-typed copy), we can read the mark and tell which purchase they come from. We keep no list linking a mark to a name. This is what article 9 of the terms of use provides.

Purpose
Protecting our texts against copying, enforcing the license and defending our rights.
Legal basis
Our legitimate interest in protecting our works and fighting piracy, and performance of the license contract.
Data
The mark written into the texts shown on your phone.
Retention
The mark stays in the texts shown on your phone. The analysis of a shared text is kept for the length of the procedure, and until the end of any dispute.

Protecting the service against abuse and attacks.
Legal basis

Translating what the other wrote (optional)

If the two of you play in two languages (French and English), the app can translate what the other typed. It is off until you choose it in the Settings. The original text is never replaced: it stays one tap away under its translation, and it is what the Book keeps.

  • Offline: the translation is made on your phone, with the free Bergamot engine (Mozilla). Nothing is sent. The app downloads the translation models once (about 26 MB per language) from our server api.dejanous.love, then keeps them on the phone.
  • Online: the sentence to translate is sent to our server, which passes it to Google Translate (Google Cloud Translation) and sends you the result. Our server does not store it: it only counts the characters translated in the month, to stay within the service’s free quota. Google processes the sentence under the Cloud Translation terms. When that quota is reached, or without a network, the app translates offline.

Machine translation is approximate, mostly for idioms: the app says so under every translated text.

The app’s other connections

The app is installed and updated by Google Play. Apart from Google Play, it only contacts our server api.dejanous.love (for the key of the full game and, if you choose them, the translation models and service) and Google Drive, if you turn the backup on. It downloads no updates and no cards from anywhere else.

Like any Internet request, these connections show your IP address to the service receiving them.

Optional backup in your Google Drive

If, and only if, you turn on “Back up with Google” in “The Book of us”, the app asks for the “drive.appdata” permission: a hidden folder of your Google Drive reserved for Déjà Nous. It saves a copy of your Book there, so you can recover it after a reinstall or a new phone.

The copy is encrypted on your phone before it is sent, with a random key that is itself protected by a password you choose (PBKDF2 derivation, 600,000 iterations). That password is never sent to the publisher or to Google. Without it, nobody can read the copy. It cannot be recovered: if you forget it, you can set a new one from a phone that already has the backup turned on (it keeps the key in the app’s private space). Without that phone and without the password, the copy is permanently unreadable.

Signing in to your Google account gives access to the encrypted file, not to its content. The app cannot access any other file in your Drive, does not read your e-mail address or profile, and the publisher has no access to that folder. The copy lives in your own Google account, under your Google terms.

You can delete it at any time from the app (turn the backup off) or from your Google account settings (“Manage apps” > Déjà Nous > “Delete hidden app data”), and revoke access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Déjà Nous’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements: this data is only used to restore your Book and is neither transferred to third parties nor used for advertising.

You can ask us which ones apply.

Your rights

You have the right to access your data, to have it corrected or erased, to object to processing based on our legitimate interest, to ask for it to be restricted, and to receive the data you gave us in a common format (portability).

How to do it. Write to contact@dejanous.love. We answer within one month. We may ask you to prove your identity if we are in doubt.

What we have on you. Next to nothing: no name, no e-mail (unless you write to us), no answers. For your purchase and your Google account, Google Play is the right place to ask. We keep what the law requires us to keep, such as accounting records.

The data that stays on your phone or in your Google account is yours to view, change and delete: we have no access to it.

Complaint. If you think your rights are not respected, you can lodge a complaint with the CNIL, the French data protection authority: www.cnil.fr, 3 place de Fontenoy, TSA 80715, 75334 Paris Cedex 07, France.
